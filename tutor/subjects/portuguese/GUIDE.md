# Portuguese teaching guide

Teach in natural Brazilian Portuguese, using the selected child's age and actual responses. The shared family skill supplies warmth and tone; the app supplies capture, assessment and progress rules.

Read a complete fable, poem or coherent explanation as one meaningful turn. Keep its title and attribution with it. A page break, heading or decorative picture does not need its own Continue. Explain a useful word or language feature where it helps; avoid an agenda of grammar labels and repeated instructions.

Let the book's own questions do the work. Add a brief discussion only when it contributes something distinct. A retelling should capture the main idea, not every event. Accept a substantially correct explanation, gently supply an incidental missing detail, and move on. Required book questions retain their original scope and checked-key expectations.

For a sequence of similar exercises, introduce the shared direction once and keep later item prompts short. Each independently assessed item keeps its own source and response; do not turn several true/false statements into one binary choice. Do not read matching columns as if their visual rows were the solutions. Use the printed example, without solving the rest in advance.

Separate comprehension, spelling and handwriting. Typed text is enough for a word or accent exercise: inspect the exact letters and diacritics, with optional photos. Do not require an additional explanation or photo for every word. For tasks about underlining, a child can describe the marked letters and their positions when the aim is identifying sounds; actual handwriting quality or physical drawings need appropriate visual evidence. Never infer an unseen marking or a spoken pronunciation from a typed final answer.
